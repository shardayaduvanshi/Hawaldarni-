About The Book

For eleven years, Naina believed a promise was broken. She was twenty-one when she wrote it in her own blood — I am waiting. You promised me you will come back. Come back alive. And then he never called again, and she told herself the silence had answered her question long ago.

Then one ordinary February morning, in a small bookshop near her husband's office, a cover catches her eye — a soldier's uniform, a woman beside him, and a sky that looks strange, almost like handwriting. It is handwriting. Hers.

Hawaldarni is the story Naina was never meant to read — of the man who loved her, the wife who never knew, and the sub-inspector who loved him first and paid for it in ways no one ever thanked her for. Told through the eyes of the friend who finally wrote it all down, this is a novel about the debts we spend our whole lives trying to repay, the promises we keep long after the people we made them to have stopped waiting — and the one letter that finally answers, eleven years too late.

This is not just a story. It is a flow of emotions, a depth of love and pain rarely put into words. It touches something in you that you didn't know was still waiting to be touched — a grief you thought was only yours, a love you thought no one else had felt quite this way. You will find some part of yourself in it. Once you step into it, you will not find it easy to step back out.

How much it was. She would spend the rest of her life understanding exactly that.

If you have ever loved someone
and never said all of it —
this book is about you.
